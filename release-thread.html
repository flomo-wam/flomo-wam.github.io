Release thread

3D Scene Flow as a World-Action Model Intermediate for Learning from Human Video

  1. 01

    Opening

    Video-pretrained robot policies don’t need to predict video. FloMo predicts 3D motion + actions instead of future pixels. Add RGB back to the same 5B model, and success drops 87%→40% ID and 80%→0% OOD. Video priors belong in the weights—not the target. Introducing FloMo 🧵
  2. 02

    1 / 7

    [1/7] Why motion? Future RGB entangles dynamics with texture, reflections, and lighting. Scene flow strips away appearance and camera motion, retaining 3D displacement. In a controlled probe with 1% action data, ground-truth motion yields 22% lower action error than RGB.
  3. 03

    2 / 7

    [2/7] How do we plug 3D motion into a video model without a new tokenizer? Render (Δx, Δy, Δz) as RGB, encode it with the frozen Wan 2.2 VAE, then LoRA-finetune its 5B DiT to jointly flow-match scene flow + actions. Video priors stay in the weights—not the target.
  4. 04

    3 / 7

    [3/7] A clean ablation: same pretrained backbone and training data, now add future-RGB prediction to motion + action. Average success across 3 contact-rich tasks falls from 87% to 40%. More supervision is not always better: pixel prediction interferes with policy learning.

  5. 05

    4 / 7

    [4/7] Under distribution shift, the gap widens. Across unseen objects, motion primitives, and tasks, FloMo succeeds on 12/15 real-robot rollouts (80%). The matched model that also predicts future RGB succeeds on 0/15. Removing the appearance target recovers generalization.

  6. 06

    5 / 7

    [5/7] Where does that OOD behavior come from? Remove only ~81h of action-free human video—keeping FloMo and its motion target fixed—and average OOD success falls from 80% to 7%. Those novel motion classes appear in human video, not robot training data.
  7. 07

    6 / 7

    [6/7] When both targets are available, which does the action stream use? In FloMo w/ Video, action queries shift attention toward motion across the DiT. Over 1,000 held-out samples, they attend ~3× more to motion than video—an ordering consistent across both robot datasets.
  8. 08

    7 / 7

    [7/7] What should a world-action model predict? Our results point to motion—not pixels. Video priors can live in the weights; 3D scene flow gives the action head a direct, transferable interface to those priors. Paper + project page + rollouts: https://flomo-wam.github.io
