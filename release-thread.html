3D Scene Flow as a World Action Model Intermediate for Learning from Human Video

Use the attachment links below each post. Copy buttons copy the text only.

  1. 01

    1 / 8 · Motivation

    [1/8] We built FloMo to learn robot skills from human video. It starts from a pretrained video model, but predicts 3D motion and actions instead of future RGB frames. On our three real-robot tasks, that gave 91% success, versus 40% for video + action.
  2. 02

    2 / 8 · Representation

    [2/8] We represent motion as 3D scene flow: how each point moves relative to the first frame. We map the x/y/z displacements to RGB channels and use the video model's frozen VAE to encode them. This lets us reuse its latent space without training a new tokenizer.
  3. 03

    3 / 8 · Model and training

    [3/8] We train a Wan 2.2 5B backbone on human videos and robot demos together. Human clips supply motion targets without action labels. Robot demos supply both. We then fine-tune on robot data alone, keeping the VAE and text encoder frozen.
  4. 04

    4 / 8 · In-distribution results

    [4/8] We tested the prediction target with the same architecture and training setup. Across oven, toolbox and corn tasks (15 trials each): Motion + action: 91% Video + motion + action: 51% Video + action: 40% Adding RGB prediction made the policy worse.
  5. 05

    5 / 8 · OOD results

    [5/8] We also tested an unseen motion, task and object: 60% success (18/30), vs 20% each for DreamZero and AMPLIFY. Here's string pulling, which wasn't in the robot training data. FloMo succeeded in 7/10 trials; the best comparison baseline was 1/10.
  6. 06

    6 / 8 · RoboTwin results

    [6/8] On the five RoboTwin Easy tasks we tested, FloMo had the highest success on each. Average success: FloMo 63.6% π0.5 44.8% FAST-WAM 41.0% Diffusion Policy 19.2% Each policy used 50 demos per task and was evaluated on 100 episodes per task.
  7. 07

    7 / 8 · Egocentric human video

    [7/8] Removing the human videos dropped OOD success from 60% (18/30) to 7% (1/15), with the same model and motion target. We removed ~81h of EgoVerse and 0.7h of in-house human demos. This suggests the human data accounts for much of the transfer.
  8. 08

    8 / 8 · Why motion?

    [8/8] Two clues to why motion helps: with ground-truth futures and 1% action data, motion gave ~22% lower action error than RGB. In the joint video/motion model, action tokens attended ~3× more to motion than video. Paper and demos: https://flomo-icra.github.io/
