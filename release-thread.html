3D Scene Flow as a World-Action Model Intermediate for Learning from Human Video

  1. 01

    Opening

    Robot policies still need to see the scene. But when learning to act, how the scene moves can be more useful than how its future frames look. FloMo keeps a video-pretrained backbone and changes the prediction target from future RGB to 3D scene flow. Introducing FloMo 🧵

    Current RGB observation · 2D tracks · 3D scene flow

  2. 02

    1 / 6

    [1/6] FloMo still sees the current RGB image. Our claim is about the training target. Future RGB mixes motion with texture, lighting, and background. 3D scene flow focuses prediction on how the scene changes, making it more directly useful for action.
  3. 03

    2 / 6

    [2/6] How do we feed 3D scene flow to a video model? We render cumulative 3D displacement in a canonical frame as an RGB-like video, then encode it with the frozen Wan 2.2 VAE. No new tokenizer: one 5B DiT jointly flow-matches scene-flow and action tokens with LoRA.
  4. 04

    3 / 6

    [3/6] We train on action-free human video and robot teleoperation, then test on three contact-rich tasks: FloMo 87% Joint Video-Action 47% FloMo w/ Video 40% BC from scratch 7% Adding future RGB to FloMo lowers average ID success from 87% to 40%.
  5. 05

    4 / 6

    [4/6] OOD success across 15 real-robot rollouts: Close cabinet (unseen primitive): 5/5 Pull string (unseen task): 3/5 Highlighter (unseen object): 4/5 FloMo: 12/15 (80%) Joint Video-Action: 20% DreamZero: 27% FloMo w/ Video: 0%

    Pull string · unseen task

  6. 06

    5 / 6

    [5/6] Using motion instead of video prediction lets FloMo make much better use of human video. Remove ~81h of action-free human video while keeping FloMo and its scene-flow target fixed: OOD success falls from 80% to 7%. Those videos contain motions absent from robot data.
  7. 07

    6 / 6

    [6/6] Two diagnostics: At 1% of probe data, ground-truth motion gives 22% lower action error than ground-truth RGB. Action queries attend ~3× more to motion than video tokens. Together, they suggest motion is easier for the action head to use. Paper: flomo-wam.github.io
