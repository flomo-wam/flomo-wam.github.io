3D Scene Flow as a World Action Model Intermediate for Learning from Human Video

  1. 01

    1 / 8 · Motivation

    [1/8] What should a robot world model predict: future pixels, or how the world moves? Introducing FloMo: a video-pretrained model that jointly predicts 3D motion and robot actions—and learns from human videos with no action labels.
  2. 02

    2 / 8 · Representation

    [2/8] 3D scene flow captures how points move—not their texture or lighting. The trick: render 3D displacements as RGB and encode them with the frozen video VAE. A motion target in the video model's own latent space. No new tokenizer.

    RGB observation · 2D point tracks · 3D scene flow

  3. 03

    3 / 8 · Model and training

    [3/8] One Wan 2.2 5B backbone predicts motion + robot actions. Human clips supervise motion without action labels; robot demos connect it to control. We co-train on both, then fine-tune on robot data. Human motion becomes a training signal for robot action.

  4. 04

    4 / 8 · In-distribution results

    [4/8] On three real-robot tasks, FloMo wins every task: 91% average success. Same architecture and training, different targets: • Motion + action: 91% • Video + motion + action: 51% • Video + action: 40% Adding pixel prediction hurts. 15 rollouts per task.
  5. 05

    5 / 8 · OOD results

    [5/8] Beyond robot training: unseen motion, task and object. FloMo: 60% success (18/30). DreamZero and AMPLIFY: 20% each (6/30). The standout: pulling a string—a motion absent from robot training. FloMo succeeds 7/10 times; no baseline exceeds 1/10.

  6. 06

    6 / 8 · RoboTwin results

    [6/8] It carries over to simulation. On five RoboTwin Easy tasks, FloMo leads every task: • FloMo: 63.6% • π0.5: 44.8% • FAST-WAM: 41.0% • Diffusion Policy: 19.2% 50 demos per task; 100 evaluation episodes per task and method.
  7. 07

    7 / 8 · Egocentric human video

    [7/8] Does the human video matter? Remove it, keep the model and motion target, and OOD success falls from 60% (18/30) to 7% (1/15). The removed data: ~81h of EgoVerse + 0.7h of human demos. Evidence that motion helps transfer human experience to robots.

  8. 08

    8 / 8 · Why motion?

    [8/8] Why motion? A ground-truth inverse-dynamics probe: ~22% lower action error than RGB at 1% action data. In the video+motion model, action attention favors motion over video by ~3×. Keep the video priors. Predict motion, not pixels. Paper + demos: https://flomo-icra.github.io/
