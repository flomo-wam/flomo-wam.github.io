Release thread

3D Scene Flow as a World-Action Model Intermediate for Learning from Human Video

  1. 01

    Opening

    What should a video-pretrained robot policy predict? Not more pixels. FloMo predicts 3D scene flow + actions: how the scene moves, not how every future frame looks. This gives human video and robot control a shared representation of motion. Introducing FloMo 🧵
  2. 02

    1 / 6

    [1/6] What do we mean by motion? FloMo represents motion as 3D scene flow: the 3D displacement of tracked points over time, expressed in the first camera frame. It keeps what moved, where, and by how much—while stripping away appearance and camera motion.

    RGB observation · 2D point tracks · 3D scene flow

  3. 03

    2 / 6

    [2/6] How do we make 3D scene flow native to a video model? Render (Δx, Δy, Δz) as RGB, encode it with the frozen Wan 2.2 VAE, then LoRA-finetune its 5B DiT to jointly predict 3D scene flow + actions. No new tokenizer. Video priors stay in the weights—not the target.
  4. 04

    3 / 6

    [3/6] FloMo learns 3D scene flow from action-free human video—and transfers it to robot control. On unseen objects, motion primitives, and tasks, FloMo succeeds on 12/15 rollouts (80%). Remove ~81h of human video—same architecture, same target—and OOD success falls to 7%.

    Average OOD success

    With human video80%
    Without human video7%

    Same architecture and 3D scene-flow target · only the human-video data changes

  5. 05

    4 / 6

    [4/6] What happens if FloMo also predicts future RGB? Performance drops. 3D scene flow + action: 87% ID, 80% OOD 3D scene flow + RGB + action: 40% ID, 0% OOD Adding pixels doesn’t complement 3D scene flow—it competes with the representation the policy uses for control.

  6. 06

    5 / 6

    [5/6] With future RGB and 3D scene flow both available, what drives action prediction? Across 1,000 held-out examples, action queries attend ~3× more to 3D scene flow than video through the DiT. The action stream favors 3D scene flow.
  7. 07

    6 / 6

    [6/6] Video teaches models how the world looks. 3D scene flow can teach them how to act. FloMo turns human and robot behavior into one language: what moves, where, and by how much. For world-action models, 3D scene flow—not pixels—is the future. flomo-wam.github.io
