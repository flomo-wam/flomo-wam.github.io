3D Scene Flow as a World Action Model Intermediate for Learning from Human Video

Collaborator handles and arXiv link remain to fill in post 7.

  1. 01

    1 / 7

    [1/7] Are videos the right objective for World Action Models? Introducing FloMo: a world action model that uses 3D motion as the link between video pretraining and robot control. It jointly predicts scene flow and robot actions.
  2. 02

    2 / 7

    [2/7] What should a robot predict about the future? FloMo predicts how points in the scene move in 3D. We render that motion as RGB scene flow, focusing the prediction target on movement rather than future appearance.
  3. 03

    3 / 7

    [3/7] Encoding motion as RGB lets us reuse the frozen video VAE—the tokenizer learned during large-scale video pretraining. Given an image and instruction, the pretrained backbone jointly predicts scene flow and robot actions.
  4. 04

    4 / 7

    [4/7] Predicting motion improves robot control. FloMo outperforms the video-predicting variants on familiar and unfamiliar real-robot tasks. It also performs best among the policies we compare in simulation.
  5. 05

    5 / 7

    [5/7] A human hand and a robot gripper look different, but can produce similar motion. Scene flow gives us a shared way to describe that motion, so human videos can teach the model even without robot action labels.
  6. 06

    6 / 7

    [6/7] Human video helps robots handle unfamiliar tasks. Training with human video improves performance on tasks beyond the robot demonstrations. The shared motion representation makes that experience useful for learning to act.
