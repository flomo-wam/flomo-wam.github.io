[1/7] World action models typically learn to act by predicting video.

We find that predicting 3D motion works better.

Introducing FloMo: a world action model that connects video pretraining to robot control through motion.

---

[2/7] Predicting video also means predicting textures and lighting.

FloMo focuses on how the scene moves: it predicts 3D scene flow—the displacement of points in the scene—and renders it as RGB video.

---

[3/7] Render motion as RGB, reuse the frozen Wan 2.2 video VAE. No new tokenizer needed.

Given an image and instruction, the pretrained 5B backbone jointly predicts scene flow and robot actions.

---

[4/7] On the bimanual YAM robot, predicting motion beats predicting video—or both—on trained tasks.

FloMo also leads the policies we compare on each evaluated RoboTwin 2.0 task, and transfers better to unfamiliar real-robot tasks.

---

[5/7] A human hand and a robot gripper look nothing alike, but can move an object in similar ways.

Scene flow captures that shared motion, letting FloMo learn from human videos without robot action labels.

---

[6/7] Human video teaches motion the robot never demonstrated.

FloMo transfers to pulling a yoyo string (unseen task) and moving a highlighter (unseen object). Removing human video sharply reduces success on these unfamiliar tasks.
