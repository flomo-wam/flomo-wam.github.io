[1/7] Are videos the right objective for World Action Models?

Introducing FloMo: a world action model that uses 3D motion as the link between video pretraining and robot control. It jointly predicts scene flow and robot actions.

---

[2/7] What should a robot predict about the future?

FloMo predicts how points in the scene move in 3D. We render that motion as RGB scene flow, focusing the prediction target on movement rather than future appearance.

---

[3/7] Encoding motion as RGB lets us reuse the frozen video VAE—the tokenizer learned during large-scale video pretraining.

Given an image and instruction, the pretrained backbone jointly predicts scene flow and robot actions.

---

[4/7] Does the prediction target matter?

In our controlled real-robot comparison, motion + actions reaches 91% average success, versus 51% for video + motion + actions and 40% for video + actions.

---

[5/7] The same motion representation also connects human video and robot demonstrations.

A human hand and a robot gripper look different, but can produce similar scene flow for the same task. Human clips can therefore supervise motion without robot action labels.

---

[6/7] Does that human video help the robot?

On out-of-distribution tasks, success rises from 7% without human video to 60% with it. Both models predict motion and actions; the difference is human-video training data.
