[1/7] Are videos the right objective for World Action Models?

Introducing FloMo: a world action model that uses 3D motion as the link between video pretraining and robot control. It jointly predicts scene flow and robot actions.

---

[2/7] We render 3D motion as RGB and encode it with the frozen video VAE, reusing the tokenizer learned during large-scale video pretraining.

Given an image and instruction, one pretrained backbone jointly predicts motion and robot actions.

---

[3/7] Motion gives us a common training target for human and robot video.

Human clips supervise motion prediction. Robot demonstrations supervise motion and actions. We then fine-tune on robot data.

---

[4/7] Predicting motion gives the highest success on every evaluated real-robot task.

Motion + actions: 91%
Video + motion + actions: 51%
Video + actions: 40%

Average success on Oven, Toolbox and Corn.

---

[5/7] Adding action-free human video improves generalization.

Across three real-robot transfer tasks, success is 60% with human video, versus 7% without it.

---

[6/7] When we train a model to predict video, motion and actions together, where do the action tokens attend?

On held-out robot data, they allocate about 3x as much attention to motion as to video, averaged across layers.
